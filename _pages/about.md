<section class="home-hero">
  <p class="profile-kicker">Continuum mechanics of soft and biological matter</p>
  <p class="home-lede">I'm Shiheng Zhao (赵世恒), a Ph.D. student at the <a href="https://www.pks.mpg.de/self-organization-of-multicellular-systems">Max Planck Institute for the Physics of Complex Systems (MPI-PKS)</a>.</p>
  <p class="research-citation-box__lead">My current research is broadly concerned with the continuum mechanics of soft and biological matter, where geometry, growth, large deformation, and activity can all influence mechanical response.</p>
  <p>I use theoretical and computational approaches to ask how soft materials and biological structures deform, become unstable, and transmit mechanical signals. This has led me to problems such as cyst indentation (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.228402">PRL 2025</a>) and boundary-driven tissue morphogenesis (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2505160122">PNAS 2025</a>), as well as questions about how geometry changes elastic instabilities, for example buckling on curved surfaces (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1103/63py-ph5s">PRL 2025</a>). In parallel, I have studied aspects of elastodynamics in soft and architected media, including wave propagation in soft fibers and lattice-based polar solids (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijsolstr.2021.111208">IJSS 2021</a>, <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2023.105226">JMPS 2023</a>). Recently, I have also become interested in possible connections with pattern formation and the physics and mechanics of nonequilibrium soft systems.</p>
</section>
